Median price paid, HM Land Registry Price Paid Data, accessed 10 August 2026. The 1935 figure is the price printed on the London board of that year, shown as a historical contrast.

The printed ladder spread its prices over 6.7×. The recorded evidence spreads them over 103×, from Old Kent Road at £325,000 to Bond Street at £33,575,353.

A board game is the most absurd possible place to insist that every price be real. That is exactly why it was built there. If the rule holds up somewhere this frivolous, it holds up in a lender covenant pack.

What this is not

Stated here rather than discovered later, which is the same discipline the gate enforces.

  • Not a valuationMedian price paid is recorded evidence, not an opinion of value. On a few streets the method mixes in some commercial transfers; Bond Street's figure is the one most affected, and each affected square says so on its face.
  • Not completeTrafalgar Square has no residential sale on record at all, so it falls back to the borough UK HPI average — and the square admits it.
  • Not one promptIt took several rounds, and independent critics rejected the first build outright. The method is the interesting part, not the speed.
