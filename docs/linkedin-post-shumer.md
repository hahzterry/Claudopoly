## THE POST

I watched Matt Shumer one-prompt a first-person shooter out of Claude — Claude of Duty — and thought: fine, but a shooter can't be wrong.

So I built the thing that can.

Twenty-two London streets. Every price on the board is the real 2026 figure, from HM Land Registry open data.

Old Kent Road: £60 on the 1935 board. £325,000 today. Still the cheapest square.

Park Lane was the second dearest in 1935. It is now the sixth cheapest.

Bond Street was twentieth of twenty-two. It is now first, at £33,575,353.

The printed ladder spanned 6.7x. The recorded evidence spans 103x.

Here is the part I actually care about.

Matt's line was "not a single external asset was used." Mine can't be that — it runs on three.js and on public Land Registry data, and pretending otherwise would be the one unverifiable claim in a post about not making them.

Mine is: not a single unsourced number was used. And unlike a boast, that one is enforced.

The game will not start if it finds a figure it cannot trace. Not a warning — it halts, names the number, and prints why. It sweeps 141 monetary values, roughly 1,700 text nodes and 99 strings baked into the 3D textures on every single load.

Then I attacked it eight times to check it wasn't decoration. An unsourced value tagged as though traced. An untagged value. A banned term. Trade dress. A fabricated figure baked into a texture where no text search would reach it. An undeclared numeral. Two invented rounded values whose entire rounding band contains no real transaction.

All eight caught. The video shows the eighth: I plant a fake £41,500,000 on Bond Street and the game refuses to load.

A board game is the most absurd possible place to enforce this. That is exactly why I chose it. Invented prices are traditional there. If the rule survives somewhere this frivolous, it survives in a lender covenant pack.

The same discipline ran over the event cards: 21 kept, each sourced to gov.uk, the Bank of England or the ONS. 14 rejected as unverifiable — including "commercial EPC C by 2027, B by 2030", which a great deal of UK CRE repeats as settled law. It is consultation, not law.

Where it falls short, in my words before they're yours: the data panel still hasn't passed its own Financial Times standards review, 71 out of 82. The median price paid method mixes some commercial transfers into a few streets — Bond Street's figure most of all — and each affected square says so on its face.

An original homage to Elizabeth Magie's The Landlord's Game, 1904, public domain. You win on rent roll, not on the size of your pile. That was her point, and the version everyone remembers inverted it.

## NAME OPTIONS

- CLAUDE OF DEEDS — mirrors his title, property-native, clean
- THE CLAUDELORD'S GAME — Magie's actual 1904 title, historically right
- CLAUDE KENT ROAD — the one square the old board still gets right
- MONOCLAUDE — evokes the genre without naming it
- CLAUDEOPOLY — punniest, but see the trade mark note below

Trade mark note: MONOPOLY is a registered Hasbro mark and the `-opoly` suffix has
been defended in court. The build's own integrity gate lists MONOPOLY as a banned
term, so a name containing it would fail the gate and refuse to start. Whichever
name is chosen, the on-board naming stays original.
